How to Learn Fast in Limited Hours

  • Active Recall over Passive Watching: Watch a Must-Watch video at 1.5x, pause, and immediately solve one PYQ on paper without looking. Writing Booth or Division trace tables from memory builds exam speed.
  • Micro-Spacing: Use the 25/5 Pomodoro bar above. Do Unit 1 + Unit 5 first, revisit 10 Part A MCQs during breaks, then tackle Unit 2 + Unit 3. Re-testing a formula 90 minutes later doubles retention.
  • Interleaving Sums: Don't solve 5 Booth sums in a row. Alternate 1 Booth, 1 Non-Restoring Division, 1 EA problem, and 1 IEEE 754 problem so your brain practices spotting which rule applies.
  • Sleep vs. All-Nighter: A 6-hour sleep block (2:00 AM – 8:00 AM) locks in binary shift procedures and prevents careless sign-bit mistakes at 2:00 PM.
  • Exam Anxiety & 40-Minute OMR Strategy: Part A is collected at minute 40. Answer the sure-shot 14–16 MCQs in the first 20 minutes, solve calculation MCQs (opcode bits, Gray code, base conversion) cleanly in the next 15, and double-check bubbles before minute 40. In Part B/C, always write the formula and draw the labeled diagram first for method marks.
